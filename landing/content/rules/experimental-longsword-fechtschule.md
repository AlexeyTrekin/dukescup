Fechtschule is about clean fencing. You have only 3 points per fight, a double knocks out both fighters, and an afterblow can't win you a fight. We don't try to reconstruct a historical fechtschule exactly: we want to keep the spirit of fights where every exchange matters, while staying close to modern tournament rules.[^thanks]

Gear, safety, judging, allowed and forbidden techniques, challenges and penalties are the same as in the main categories: see the [tournament rules]({{< relref "tournament-rules.md" >}}) and the [gear requirements]({{< relref "gear-requirements.md" >}}). This page covers only what's different.

## 1. Rounds

1. Fechtschule is one category for everyone, fought in several rounds. Each round is a complete tournament of its own, with its own winner. The number of rounds depends on the timing.
2. Before each round, the pairs are drawn at random. The round is a single-elimination bracket up to the final. If the number of fighters doesn't fill the bracket, some fighters go straight to the next stage.
3. The winner of the round's final gets a goblet.
4. A round can end without a winner: if both finalists lose the final, nobody gets the goblet.

## 2. Winning a fight

1. Each fighter starts the fight with 3 points. Your opponent's hits take your points away.
2. You win the fight when your opponent has 0 points and you still have at least 1, or when you have more points after 2 minutes of active fighting.
3. Both fighters lose when both drop to 0 at the same time, or when the points are equal at the end of the time. Then nobody from this fight goes on, and their opponent in the next stage goes on automatically. This applies at every stage, up to the final.

## 3. Points

| Action | Points taken |
|--------|:------------:|
| Cut or thrust to the head, neck or body | 3 |
| Hit to the arms or legs, including hands | 1 |
| Any hit with the sword in one hand | 1 |
| Slice to any zone | 1 |
| Controlled pommel strike to the face of the mask | 1 |
| Weapon loss | 3 from the fighter who lost the sword |
| Ring out | 1 from the fighter who left the area |
| Fall | 1 from the fighter who fell, unless the fall is part of a technical action |

If a fighter lands several hits before "Stop", only the most effective one counts.

## 4. Doubles

A double to any zone takes all the points of both fighters: both lose the fight, at any stage up to the final.

## 5. Afterblow

1. An afterblow is a hit on the fighter who hit first, made within one fencing tempo after the first hit. A fencing tempo is the time needed to perform one simple action without delay.
2. An afterblow takes exactly 1 point, whatever the zone.
3. There is no afterblow:
   - after a two-handed hit to the head or neck (after a one-handed hit there, the afterblow counts);
   - if the fighter who was hit took a step before hitting back;
   - if the fighter who was hit wound up before hitting back.
4. An afterblow can take the last point of the fighter who hit first only if the first hit leaves the opponent at 0 too. So you can't win a fight with an afterblow, but you can make both fighters lose.

Examples: the first fighter hits, the second answers with an afterblow.

| Score before | First fighter hits | Score after | Result |
|:------------:|--------------------|:-----------:|--------|
| 2 – 2 | arm or leg | 1 – 1 | the fight goes on |
| 2 – 1 | anywhere but the head | 1 – 0 | the first fighter wins |
| 1 – 1 | anywhere but the head | 0 – 0 | both lose |
| 1 – 2 | arm or leg | 1 – 1 | the fight goes on: the afterblow can't take the last point |
| 2 – 2 | body | 1 – 0 | the first fighter wins |
| 1 – 2 | body | 0 – 0 | both lose |
| 2 – 2 | head | 2 – 0 | the first fighter wins |
| 1 – 2 | head | 1 – 0 | the first fighter wins |

## 6. Penalties

The cards are the same as in the [tournament rules]({{< relref "tournament-rules.md#penalties" >}}), adapted to points being taken away:

- a red card takes 1 point from the carded fighter; if it was their last point, they lose the fight;
- a black card means the carded fighter loses the fight, and the opponent goes on.

## 7. Cleanest technique

After all the rounds, we award the fighter with the cleanest technique: the best ratio of points kept per fight across all the rounds.

- Points kept are the points you have at the end of a fight.
- Only fights that actually took place count; going on automatically is not a fight.
- To compete for this award, you need to take part in at least 80% of the rounds, rounded up.
